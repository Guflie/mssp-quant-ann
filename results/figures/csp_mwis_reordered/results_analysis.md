# Analysis of the CSP and MWIS experiments

This analysis compares the completed MWIS campaign `new_pipeline_results_reordered` with the matched CSP runs on the frozen `exp_snapshot_2026-10-12` inputs. The dominance-enhanced MWIS test is excluded. Seven distinct configurations are compared; the 200-satellite/50-gateway baseline is reused across the experimental groups rather than counted as an independent repetition. Plot links identify the generated figures so that their final figure numbers can be assigned when integrating this text into the report.

Both implemented hybrid pipelines produced conflict-free schedules with the same certified optimal objective for every matched configuration. Their final contact counts also agree. Certification applies to the finite candidate-contact model and its adopted constraints, including at most one contact per satellite over the horizon; it does not establish optimality for the unrestricted operational MMSP. The differences emerge before MILP and in the amount of work allocated to preprocessing, separator decomposition, sampling and fallback. The experiments therefore support a comparison of the complete implemented pipelines, rather than a claim that one mathematical formulation is inherently superior.

The pre-MILP gaps in this table are retrospective gaps to the certified final optimum: `100 × (final optimum − pre-MILP objective) / final optimum`. They are distinct from the looser conservative bounds available before fallback. Optimality certification follows the solver and reconstruction tolerances used by the pipeline.

The [three-stage quality plot](09_quality_through_stages.pdf) shows that CSP achieves better pre-MILP quality in every tested configuration except 500/50. In the baseline, its gap is 1.211%, compared with 2.047% for MWIS. The difference is particularly pronounced at 200/100, where the CSP gap is 0.062%, compared with 1.771% for MWIS. CSP already obtains the certified optimal objective before MILP at 100/50 and at the 200/50 terminal profile of 50/350. At 500/50, however, both approaches have their largest pre-MILP gaps, and MWIS is slightly better. The [pre-MILP gap plot](05_pre_milp_quality_gap.pdf) makes these differences easier to see than an absolute-objective plot, where the final curves coincide and small relative differences can be difficult to distinguish.

The classical processing explains an important part of this behavior. The [component-structure plot](08_component_structure.pdf) shows that, for the baseline, CSP reduces 1,228 candidate contacts to 460 contacts before component decomposition, whereas the evaluated MWIS version retains all 1,228 contacts at this stage. The reduced CSP graph contains 90 independent components, compared with 20 for MWIS, and its largest component contains 315 contacts rather than 1,189. At 100/50, CSP's largest component contains only 20 contacts and its decomposition has no incomplete leaf. These observations are consistent with the benefit of CSP's initial dominance reduction and its subsequent decomposition strategy. Their individual effects are not isolated by the experiment, because the implementations also differ in separator selection, encoding and sampling budgets.

Average terminal size alone gives an incomplete description of decomposition performance. The [leaf-size distributions](12_leaf_size_distributions.pdf) show a substantial separation between the small subproblems that reach sampling and the large unresolved pieces that remain in the trees. The 500/50 case illustrates this particularly clearly: both methods export only a few small QUBOs while retaining a very large component that requires fallback. CSP's initial reduction removes 671 of 5,224 contacts, approximately 12.8%, leaving a largest component of 4,534 contacts; the largest MWIS component contains 5,215 contacts. This case therefore demonstrates a limit of the configured decomposition procedures, despite the small dimensions of the sampled terminals.

The [pre/post-MILP provenance plot](10_pre_post_milp_provenance.pdf) distinguishes schedule quality from the source of its selected contacts. Before MILP, the original MWIS baseline has 180 contacts: 140 from greedy incomplete leaves, 26 from sampled terminals, 12 fixed during exact preprocessing and two from separator choices. Its relatively high pre-MILP objective therefore does not result primarily from annealing. After MILP replaces the incomplete component, the final MWIS schedule contains 157 MILP contacts, 12 annealed contacts and 11 contacts fixed during preprocessing. The corresponding CSP schedule contains 76 MILP contacts, 34 annealed contacts and 70 contacts fixed during preprocessing. CSP retains more annealing contributions and depends less on MILP in this baseline, while exact classical preprocessing remains a major contributor.

This distinction becomes more important at 500/50. Both pipelines retain only three annealed contacts in their final schedules. MILP supplies 459 of 464 contacts for MWIS and 449 of 464 for CSP. Reaching the global optimum in this case demonstrates the effectiveness of the complete hybrid pipeline, but it cannot be attributed mainly to the sampled terminals. Provenance measures the origins of contacts retained in the output, rather than CPU effort: an annealed choice discarded by branch selection or replaced by MILP still incurred sampling cost. The [sampling and decomposition workload plot](07_sampling_and_decomposition.pdf) should therefore be read alongside provenance.

The terminal-size experiment reveals different responses to relaxing the logical limits. The [sampling cost–quality plot](11_terminal_profile_cost_quality.pdf) compares the three profiles on the identical frozen 200/50 input. For CSP, moving from 20/100 to 50/350 increases the sampled QUBOs from 166 to 555 and total reads from 166,000 to 555,000. Its pre-MILP gap decreases from 1.216% to zero, and incomplete leaves decrease from 153 to 40. The final schedule nevertheless retains 19 sampled terminals and 34 annealed contacts in all three profiles, because the incomplete component is subsequently replaced by MILP. The larger profile improves the quality of the intermediate reconstruction without increasing the retained annealing contribution in the final output. Since unresolved alternative branches remain, an optimal incumbent before MILP does not by itself certify the entire decomposition tree.

MWIS responds much less strongly to the same change in limits. Its sampled QUBO count increases from 29 to 31, and its pre-MILP gap decreases only from 2.047% to 2.017%. This limited change indicates that the configured separator search and decomposition budgets restrict progress before the larger terminal capacity can be used extensively. It does not establish that larger QUBOs are intrinsically unhelpful to MWIS. Both limits change together, and total sampling work depends on the resulting tree, so the experiment does not isolate the effect of logical variables, couplers or read count individually.

The [three-stage quality plot](09_quality_through_stages.pdf) also establishes an important limit on the interpretation of annealing. In all 14 method/configuration records, the sampled reconstruction matches the objective of the classical tree reference. That reference solves terminal problems exactly and uses the same greedy solutions for incomplete leaves. This demonstrates matching reference quality at the reconstructed-schedule level; equality of the aggregate objective does not establish that every sampled terminal in every alternative branch was solved optimally. No objective improvement over classical tree evaluation is observed. Since actual quantum hardware was not used, the runs provide no evidence of a quantum performance advantage. The [logical-dimension plot](04_logical_qubo_dimensions.pdf) describes binary variables and couplers only, rather than physical embedding size, chain lengths or hardware overhead.

The [runtime breakdown](03_runtime_breakdown.pdf) shows that runtime is strongly affected by decomposition behavior, rather than increasing monotonically with satellite or gateway count. The recorded baseline notebook times sum to approximately 683.6 seconds for MWIS and 32.4 seconds for CSP. At 500/50, the corresponding times are approximately 86.9 and 2,224.1 seconds. The logs show that MWIS stops after 39 tree nodes because further separators are not found within its configured search limits, whereas CSP explores 7,468 tree nodes and encounters recursion-depth and separator-state limits. Thus, the shorter MWIS runtime at 500/50 coincides with much less decomposition work and substantial fallback dependence. It should not be interpreted as evidence that annealing solved the larger instance more efficiently. These times are descriptive: hardware/runtime provenance was not matched, per-terminal budgets differ (500 MWIS reads versus 1,000 CSP reads), and the full CSP 100/50 runtime was not saved. The plotted totals sum notebook elapsed times and exclude outer orchestration and frozen-input generation.

Scaling results also require attention to input composition. In the gateway experiment, increasing the gateway count from 25 to 50 changes 37 members of the selected 200-satellite set; increasing it from 50 to 100 changes another 11 members. The final objective decreases from 12,895.044 at 25 gateways to 12,581.450 at 50 gateways, then increases to 13,058.865 at 100 gateways. This is an observed difference among the selected scenarios, rather than evidence that adding ground resources reduces achievable utility. Although station subsets are nested in this sweep, the satellite sets are not held identical. The runs are paired fairly between methods within each configuration, but they do not form a clean causal experiment on gateway count alone.

The evidence supports a bounded, instance-dependent benefit from the CSP implementation: it generally reduces the candidate graph more strongly, improves pre-MILP quality and retains more annealed contacts than the evaluated MWIS implementation. Both pipelines reach the same certified final quality with classical fallback, and both rely heavily on MILP in the largest satellite scenario. These findings describe the division of work among exact reductions, sampling, greedy completion and MILP. Establishing controlled runtime superiority, isolating formulation or encoding effects, or demonstrating a quantum advantage would require matched budgets and environments, repeated trials and, for quantum claims, actual hardware measurements.
